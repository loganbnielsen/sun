Decide the initial hosted billing model.

**Question:** How does billing map to projects, environments, services, usage, and managed resources?

**Context:** Billing is out of scope for the hosted executor spike, but the runtime tenancy, managed resource, and environment model can paint Sun into a corner if billing dimensions are ignored too long.

**Options:**

1. Per project/environment base fee plus usage.
2. Per service plus managed resource usage.
3. Infrastructure-pass-through plus platform fee.
4. Simple tiered plans for the first hosted version.

**Decision criteria:**

- Easy to explain to startup customers.
- Compatible with local dev being free/open.
- Measurable from the control plane and observability stack.
- Does not require premature fine-grained metering.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- A written decision chooses the first billing shape.
- The decision names the minimum usage/resource events the hosted plane must record.
- The decision explicitly states what is deferred.

**Decision:**

For very early hosted adopters, use simple cost-plus billing with guardrails:
aggregate the cloud/provider costs attributable to the customer environment and
charge a clear markup, initially around 2x observed cloud cost unless a customer
agreement says otherwise.

This is an early-adopter billing model, not the long-term packaging. It keeps
pricing understandable while Sun learns real usage patterns and avoids premature
fine-grained metering.

Customers must have an account and payment method before hosted resources are
created. Hosted mode should enforce spend controls so a customer cannot run up a
large bill and disappear.

**Implications:**

- The hosted plane needs customer/account ownership for every environment.
- The hosted plane needs spend caps, budget alerts, and suspension/approval
  thresholds before broad availability.
- The minimum billing records are environment ownership, provisioned resources,
  provider cost attribution, release/runtime activity, and billing period totals.
- Pay-as-you-go is acceptable for early adopters only with conservative caps.
- Long-term pricing can evolve into tiers plus usage once real cost drivers are
  understood.
- Fine-grained per-service metering and polished invoices are deferred.
